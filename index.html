05 — Look closer

The everyday compromises

Everyday atta may carry compromises you never see.

01

The First Compromise: Wheat Selection

The first decision is also the first compromise

Good wheat is expensive. When procurement is driven mainly by cost, what enters the mill may already be compromised.

The Atulyash Way

Atulyash begins with carefully selected high grade MP Sharbati wheat, no casual mixing, no cheaper blends.

02

The Second Compromise: Milling Stone

Stone-ground does not always mean natural stone

Most mills, including most neighbourhood chakkis, use man-made synthetic emery stones, manufactured by bonding abrasive mineral particles into hard grinding discs. Large-scale systems may use steel rollers.

The Atulyash Way

We use natural stone chakkis, no emery-stone grinding, no industrial steel rollers.

03

The Third Compromise: Milling Speed

Speed increases output. But it also increases heat.

High-speed systems commonly process hundreds of kilos per hour. Speed creates friction. Friction creates heat. And heat can affect the grain’s natural oils, aroma and character.

The Atulyash Way

Atulyash mills slowly, around 20–30 kg per hour, to keep the process gentle and controlled.

04

The Fourth Compromise: Chokar

When chokar is removed, atta loses its soul.

In many milling systems, the wheat grain is separated in stages. Bran/chokar may be removed to make atta appear smoother, improve shelf stability, or be sold separately for other food and commercial uses.

The Atulyash Way

We do not separate. Chokar remains. Nothing removed. Nothing added. Just atta in its purest form.

05

The Fifth Compromise: Discipline After Milling

After milling, atta is often treated casually.

In many milling environments, atta remains exposed during collection, handling and packing. Hands, surfaces and air become part of the environment around it.

The Atulyash Way

Respect for atta does not end with milling. Atulyash follows structured post-milling handling with minimal human contact, automated packing and closed systems in clean, monitored environments.

06

The Sixth Compromise: Shelf Life

Freshness slowly becomes secondary.

Once wheat is milled, its natural oils begin interacting with air. Over time, slow oxidation gradually changes the atta’s aroma and character. Yet most atta is made to travel, sit and stay stable for weeks or months.

The Atulyash Way

Atulyash mills in smaller quantities and delivers weekly, closer to consumption. No bulk storage, no long-stored atta.

What makes Atulyash different from regular packaged atta?+

Atulyash is built around carefully selected MP Sharbati wheat, natural stone slow milling, chokar retention and weekly delivery. The idea is not to store atta for long, but to receive it closer to consumption.

Why do you deliver weekly instead of monthly?+

Atta is eaten every day, yet it is usually bought like monthly stock. Atulyash delivers weekly so families receive smaller, fresher quantities instead of storing atta for weeks.

Is every chakki made from natural stone?+

No. Most chakkis use man-made synthetic emery stones, and large-scale systems may use steel rollers. Atulyash uses natural stone chakkis, no emery-stone grinding and no industrial steel rollers.

What is chokar, and why does it matter?+

Chokar is the natural bran of wheat. It is the soul of atta. Atulyash does not separate the grain; the natural chokar remains, making the atta fuller and closer to the whole wheat grain.

Do I have to subscribe immediately?+

No. You can start with your first Atulyash experience before moving to weekly subscription. Subscription is recommended for freshness and regularity, but the first step can be simple.
